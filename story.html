A pitch is a sequence, not a single number

A short guide to the events and measurements behind the charts

Pitching summaries often reduce a delivery to a speed or a peak. Motion-capture data lets us look at how measurements change across the recorded movement. These events help orient the plots; the dataset does not label every point as a universal phase of pitching.

Three moments to keep in mind

  1. Foot plant (FP) is the point when the stride foot contacts the ground. The dataset includes some body positions and angles at this event.
  2. Maximum external rotation (MER) is a shoulder-position milestone in the dataset. The elbow flexion measure at MER describes one joint angle at that moment; it is not itself a measure of injury.
  3. Ball release (BR) is the delivery endpoint represented in several pitch-level measures. The full-signal charts show values across the recorded timeline, rather than only at these named events.

From movement to a chart

The Explore page pairs shoulder internal/external rotation velocity with elbow varus/valgus moment at matching timestamps. Its time-course view then aligns trials by relative recorded duration. This makes the broad shape of the measured signal easier to compare, but it does not mean every pitch has identical timing or that a relative-time point corresponds exactly to a named event above.

The pitch comparison switches perspective: each point represents one trial, using its pitch speed and peak elbow varus moment. Looking at both views helps distinguish a changing signal within a pitch from differences between pitches.

Note

An elbow moment is a biomechanical estimate of joint loading, not a direct measurement of tissue damage. No injury outcomes are included in these data.
