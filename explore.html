Shoulder rotation and elbow loading during a pitch

Time-resolved mechanics from Driveline's OpenBiomechanics Project

How do throwing mechanics relate to elbow loading? This scatterplot compares shoulder rotation velocity and elbow varus/valgus moment throughout pitching motion-capture trials.

What this can—and cannot—tell us

These measurements can help explore how mechanics and joint loading vary together and may inform hypotheses about injury risk. The dataset does not include confirmed injury diagnoses, so it cannot establish which athletes were injured or validate an injury-risk estimate.

Shoulder rotation and elbow moment

Figure: Shoulder rotation velocity and elbow moment at matched time points during pitching trials.

This scatterplot helps show whether shoulder rotation speed and elbow moment tend to vary together during pitching. A pattern in the points can suggest an association between these mechanics, but it does not show that one causes the other or that either measure predicts injury.

The chart contains 247,709 matched time samples from 411 pitching trials. Marker-derived signals are sampled at 360 Hz, so each pitch contributes many measurements over time. Samples from the same pitch are repeated observations, not independent pitches.

Pitch speed and stride length are available in the project's pitch-level summary data, alongside peak shoulder-rotation velocity and elbow varus moment. This chart focuses on the time-varying mechanics instead.

How elbow moment varies during a pitch

The next plot shows how elbow varus/valgus moment changes over the recorded duration of a pitch. Curves are aligned by each trial's relative time and summarized with the median and interquartile range. This is an elbow loading measure, not a direct measurement of stress on shoulder tissue.

Figure: Median elbow varus/valgus moment over the relative recorded duration of a pitch; shading shows the interquartile range across pitches.

This time-course plot adds context by showing when elbow moment tends to rise or fall over a pitch, while the shaded band shows how much the middle half of trials varies at each point. It helps locate phases of greater typical elbow loading and compare consistency across pitches; it does not measure shoulder tissue stress or establish injury risk.

Distribution of peak elbow varus moment

Figure: Distribution of each pitch's peak signed elbow varus moment; the dashed line marks the median across pitches.

This plot shows how peak elbow moments are distributed across pitches, with one value per pitch so trials with more recorded time samples do not dominate the distribution. It compares peak elbow loading between trials, not how load is distributed across shoulder tissues or how much stress an individual shoulder experiences.

About the data

The data come from Driveline Baseball Research & Development's OpenBiomechanics Project. The repository documents 411 pitching trials, not 40,000 distinct pitches. The full-signal tables used here contain 247,709 matched timestamps across those trials; the chart includes samples where both measurements are present. The pitch-level table includes pitch speed, stride length, and summary loading metrics.
